## Design Direction — Playful, Bold, and Research-Driven

### Overall Aesthetic

The GIS Day presentation should follow TripLibre's existing **playful, colorful, and approachable design identity**.

Think:

- Playful consumer app presentation
- Bold typography and expressive layouts
- Friendly illustrations and simplified diagrams
- Colorful, memorable visual storytelling
- Professional research content presented in an engaging way

**Playful does not mean childish.**

The presentation should feel like a researcher proudly demonstrating something they built, rather than delivering a conventional academic lecture or corporate pitch.

### 1. Follow TripLibre's Existing Design Language

Refer to the official TripLibre UI design documentation and actual app screenshots.

Reuse recognizable visual elements where appropriate:

- Rounded cards and containers
- Bold outlines
- Hard or offset shadows
- Bright, cheerful accent colors
- Friendly rounded typography
- Simple iconography
- Playful geometric decorations

Avoid inventing an unrelated visual identity.

Use TripLibre's actual design tokens and colors where available.

### 2. Visual Storytelling

Every slide should have one dominant visual idea.

Examples:

**Where It All Started**

Show a playful illustrated paper travel diary, perhaps with a simple map, pencil, and route.

**From Data to Research**

Show a real research finding inside a friendly, simplified data visualization. Preserve the accuracy of the underlying result.

**Seeing the Other Side**

Use a map with an illustrative wandering GPS trace to show how difficult travel observation can be.

**Meet TripLibre**

Use oversized phone mockups and actual application screenshots. This should feel like a product reveal.

**I Couldn't Build This Alone**

Introduce the four contributors as playful character-like cards:

- Zhanchao — The Researcher / Product Owner
- ChatGPT — The Thinker
- Claude Code — The Builder
- Codex — The Reviewer

Use recognizable icons and distinct visual identities, but avoid suggesting that AI systems are autonomous employees.

**How I Got Started**

Use three large, colorful cards:

Describe → Prototype → Iterate

Make the process visually approachable, almost like a simple board game.

**One Feature, One Development Story**

Present the development process as a short visual comic or storyboard:

Issue → Build → Review → Fix

Use authentic development evidence alongside the illustrated progression.

**Sometimes, Less Is Better**

Show two competing product approaches using a playful split-screen comparison.

**What I Learned**

Use three memorable takeaway cards, not a dense summary of technical details.

### 3. Colors and Typography

Use the TripLibre design system as the primary source of truth.

Favor:

- Bright but controlled accent colors
- Warm, light backgrounds
- High-contrast dark text
- Rounded sans-serif typography
- Large expressive headlines
- Occasional oversized numbers or keywords

Avoid:

- Generic corporate blue gradients
- Dark developer-dashboard aesthetics
- Dense academic poster layouts
- Excessive neon or glowing effects
- Overly decorative illustrations that distract from the message

### 4. Motion and Interaction

Use subtle, playful transitions where they improve storytelling.

Examples:

- Cards appearing in sequence
- Route lines drawing across a map
- A phone mockup sliding into view
- Simple progress indicators
- Small visual reactions when revealing a development mistake or correction

Keep animation short and purposeful.

Do not make important information depend on animation.

All slides must remain understandable as static images.

### 5. Layout Principles

The presentation is designed for a projected 16:9 screen.

- One major idea per slide.
- One primary visual per slide.
- Minimal supporting text.
- Generous spacing.
- Large, readable headlines.
- Strong contrast.
- No scrolling during live presentation.
- No dense paragraphs or complex tables.

Vary compositions to maintain visual interest: full-screen product screenshots, split layouts, illustrated timelines, and oversized headline slides.

Do not use the same three-card layout for every slide.

### 6. Tone and Personality

The presentation can be funny, self-aware, and occasionally surprising.

Small moments of humor are welcome, especially when explaining:

- Being a transportation researcher rather than an iOS developer.
- Discovering unexpected bugs during testing.
- AI confidently producing incorrect code.
- Choosing a simpler feature instead of an unnecessarily sophisticated system.

However, humor should arise from real experiences. Do not fabricate anecdotes or exaggerate AI capabilities.

The presentation should communicate curiosity, experimentation, and intellectual independence.

### 7. Design Quality Standard

The final result should feel like a cohesive, thoughtfully designed product presentation.

It should not resemble:

- A default PowerPoint template.
- A corporate consulting presentation.
- A technical documentation website.
- A children's educational slideshow.
- A collection of unrelated AI-generated illustrations.

The goal is:

**Playful enough to be memorable. Clear enough to be academic. Polished enough to represent TripLibre.**

Use the existing TripLibre app UI as the visual anchor throughout the presentation.
